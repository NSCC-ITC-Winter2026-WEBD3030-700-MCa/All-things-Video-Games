## Strategy

Strategy games challenge players to outthink opponents through planning, resource management, positioning, and long-term decision-making. Victory comes not from reflexes alone but from foresight, adaptability, and understanding of game systems. The genre spans from casual puzzle-like experiences to deeply complex simulations requiring hundreds of hours to master.

## Subgenres

- **Real-Time Strategy (RTS)** — Players build bases, gather resources, and command armies in real time. Emphasizes multitasking, speed, and tactical execution under pressure.
- **Turn-Based Strategy (TBS)** — Players take turns making decisions, allowing time for careful deliberation. Focuses on strategic depth over mechanical speed.
- **4X (eXplore, eXpand, eXploit, eXterminate)** — Grand-scale strategy where players build civilizations or empires across large maps with complex systems.
- **Tower Defense** — Players place defensive structures to stop waves of enemies, focusing on positioning, upgrades, and resource allocation.
- **Grand Strategy** — Deep, long-term strategy focusing on diplomacy, politics, economics, and large-scale warfare across centuries.
- **MOBA (Multiplayer Online Battle Arena)** — Team-based competitive games where players control single heroes within a larger strategic framework.
- **City Builder** — Focus on constructing and managing settlements, balancing growth, infrastructure, and citizen needs.
- **Tactics** — Small-scale combat focusing on unit positioning, cover, and individual abilities rather than base building or economy.

## History

Strategy gaming traces back to tabletop roots — chess and wargames informed early digital designs. The 1980s saw titles like *Reach for the Stars* (1983) and *Nether Earth* (1987) establish foundational concepts. *Civilization* (1991) by Sid Meier defined the 4X genre and proved that deep strategy could achieve mass appeal.

The 1990s explosion of RTS games was led by *Dune II* (1992), which established the genre template, followed by *Command & Conquer* (1995) and *StarCraft* (1998). *StarCraft* in particular became a cultural phenomenon, especially in South Korea where it launched competitive gaming as a mainstream spectator sport.

The 2000s brought refinement and hybridization. *Rome: Total War* (2004) merged turn-based empire management with real-time battles. *Defense of the Ancients* (a Warcraft III mod) spawned the MOBA genre. *Civilization IV* (2005) and *Galactic Civilizations II* (2006) pushed 4X deeper.

Modern strategy games continue to evolve. *Crusader Kings III* (2020) emphasizes narrative-driven grand strategy. *Into the Breach* (2018) distills tactics into tight, puzzle-like encounters. Indies like *Northgard* (2018) and *Wartales* (2023) bring fresh takes to established formulas.

## Core Gameplay Mechanics

- **Resource Management** — Gathering, allocating, and prioritizing limited resources (gold, minerals, food, energy) to fuel expansion and military power.
- **Unit Control** — Directing individual units or armies, managing formations, abilities, and positioning in combat.
- **Base Building** — Constructing and upgrading structures that enable unit production, research, defense, and economy.
- **Tech Trees** — Branching progression systems where players choose research paths that unlock new units, buildings, and abilities.
- **Diplomacy** — Negotiating with AI or human opponents through alliances, trades, threats, and treaties.
- **Map Awareness** — Understanding terrain, fog of war, choke points, and resource locations to inform strategic decisions.
- **Fog of War** — Limited visibility forcing players to scout and make decisions with incomplete information.

## Notable Franchises

- **Civilization** — The definitive 4X series spanning over 30 years, guiding civilizations from ancient times to the future.
- **StarCraft** — The RTS benchmark for competitive balance and asymmetric faction design.
- **Age of Empires** — Historical RTS focusing on real-world civilizations and their unique units and technologies.
- **Total War** — Combining turn-based campaign strategy with massive real-time battles.
- **Crusader Kings** — Grand strategy focused on dynasty management, character relationships, and medieval politics.
- **XCOM** — Turn-based tactical combat against alien invaders with deep base management and permadeath.
- **Company of Heroes** — WWII RTS emphasizing squad-based tactics, cover mechanics, and destructible environments.
- **League of Legends / Dota 2** — The two dominant MOBAs, each with massive global player bases and esports scenes.

## Strategy in Esports

Strategy games have a rich competitive history. *StarCraft: Brood War* was the first game to establish professional esports in South Korea during the 2000s, with televised matches and celebrity players. *StarCraft II* carried this legacy internationally. MOBAs now dominate strategy esports — *League of Legends* World Championship and *The International* (Dota 2) are among the most-watched gaming events globally, with prize pools reaching tens of millions of dollars. *Age of Empires II* has experienced a remarkable esports resurgence, driven by its definitive edition release and dedicated community.
